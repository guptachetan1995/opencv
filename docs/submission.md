## Inspiration

A nine-person surveying firm photographs its receipts, and finance discovers the bad ones at
month end — four to thirty days later, when the surveyor is on another site and the paper is
in a bin. Everyone treats that as an OCR problem and buys an OCR product. It is not. OCR runs
on a capture that was already unusable and reports the failure at the wrong moment to the
wrong person. The expensive quantity is **the latency between a bad capture and anyone
knowing it was bad** — because that latency is what makes the defect permanent. Two seconds
after the shutter the photo can simply be taken again.

The second thing that pushed the design: the two possible errors do not cost the same.
Nagging someone for a retake they did not need costs seconds. Silently accepting an
unreadable receipt costs the whole claim, and the person who pays is not the person who made
the error.

## What it does

**Second Look** is a receipt-capture inspection desk that runs at the moment of capture. A
phone posts a photo; OpenCV 5 measures it — document quad and rectification, per-tile focus,
exposure and contrast, specular glare, text presence and coverage, frame-edge crop, QR or
barcode, and a perceptual hash for duplicates — and a deterministic policy turns those
measurements into exactly one of three verdicts: **accept**, **retake**, or **escalate**.

What makes it agentic rather than a filter is that the measurement chooses the *next call*. A
shot with glare across the total is asked to be re-taken with the light moved. The retake that
removes the glare but clips the bottom edge gets a **different** instruction, from a
**different** rule, fired by a **different** metric. And the agent may auto-accept only when
every metric is comfortably inside its band; the uncertain band, a defect that survived two
retakes, a suspected duplicate, and anything that does not look like a document all go to a
person — who is also the only party who can discard a capture or overturn a verdict.

## How we built it

- **OpenCV 5** (`opencv-python-headless==5.0.0.93`, pinned exactly so a loose constraint
  cannot resolve backwards into the 4.x line where warping numerics differ). Eight
  measurements over the *rectified* page, so defects are reported by location on the receipt
  rather than as one score for the photograph.
- **A committed threshold table** (`policy.toml`): an 11-rule cascade, first match wins, no
  model and no randomness. Every clause evaluated is written into the verdict's `firings`, so
  a verdict is auditable after the fact.
- **One chokepoint.** `agent_loop.invoke(tool, args, actor)` is the only way to mutate
  anything. Its first act is an actor guard that raises `PermissionError` when an agent
  reaches for a human verb or a human reaches for an agent tool. The review page's Approve
  button calls exactly the same function the agent's tools call — there is no second path to
  the accepted state.
- **AWS**: one Lambda function as a container image on arm64/Graviton2, 2048 MB, Python 3.13,
  behind a Lambda Function URL. No ALB, no API Gateway, nothing billing while idle. App Runner
  is unavailable (closed to new customers 30 April 2026) and ECS Express Mode was considered
  and rejected for cost and for requiring CI.
- **Python 3.13, standard library only for serving.** No framework, no bundler; one static
  HTML page with vanilla JavaScript.

## Challenges we ran into

- **Glare that matters versus glare that does not.** Area alone is the wrong metric: a bright
  patch on white space must be accepted, and one across the total must not.
  `glare_over_text_frac` — glare intersected with the text mask — is what separates them, and
  both cases are held by fixtures.
- **Duplicate detection colliding with itself.** dHash runs on the rectified page, so every
  defect variant of the same synthetic layout hashes close to an already-accepted capture; in
  one shared batch, six samples escalate as suspected duplicates. It is measured, written up
  as failure case 1, and never resolved by silently loosening the threshold — escalation is
  the safe direction, and a person decides.
- **Making "waits for human approval" real rather than rhetorical.** The mechanism is that
  `process_capture` is a no-op once a capture is escalated: nothing advances it until a person
  calls `invoke(..., actor="reviewer")`. No timer, no eventual auto-approve.
- **Documenting what was *not* built.** The pre-code design (not published here) prescribed
  seven things that were never built — see
  [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md#where-this-diverges-from-the-pre-code-design)'s
  divergence table. Both diagrams are drawn as-built with a legend — solid = built
  and tested, dashed = planned — because misrepresenting capability is an explicit rejection
  ground for this competition.

## What we learned

Choosing the *headline* metric first changed the design more than any threshold did. Once the
silent-accept rate is the number you report first, escalating rules must sit above retake
rules in the cascade, an uncertain band has to exist, and a suspected duplicate can never be
auto-rejected. The evaluation reports **0/15 (0.0%)** silent accepts on the synthetic set —
and reports alongside it that escalation precision of 100% is trivially high on fixtures that
are bad by construction.

## Agentic Vision Award

The award requires that "image or video results must influence a subsequent plan, tool call,
action, or request for human approval… the visual evidence must change what the system does
next." The qualifying beat, asserted end to end by `tools/run_demo.py` and measured in
[`evaluation.md`](./evaluation.md)'s retake-convergence set:

`glare_text.jpg` → `glare_over_text_frac` over threshold → rule 6 `glare_over_total` →
**retake**. The retake fixes the glare but crops the page → `edge_touch_sides` contains
`bottom` and the bottom band has text → rule 5 `bottom_edge_clipped` → **retake again, from a
different rule chosen by a different metric on a different image**. Then `clean_a.jpg` is
accepted. Nothing about the second instruction was scheduled; the second image's own
measurements selected it.

Two more things the rubric asks for, both drawn in
[`agent-workflow.md`](./agent-workflow.md): the agent **declines to keep guessing** —
`attempt >= 3` fires `persistent_defect` and escalates rather than requesting a third retake —
and **human control is enforced at the chokepoint**, where `approve`, `reject` and
`resolve_duplicate` raise `PermissionError` for any actor that is not `"reviewer"`, and
`approve` additionally refuses a suspected duplicate until `resolve_duplicate` has run.

Observability: every call writes a `TraceEntry(seq, actor, action, inputs, outputs,
caused_by)`, and `trace_chain()` walks `parent_id` back to the root capture so the whole
glare → crop → accept chain reads as one numbered sequence with `caused_by` pointing at the
measurement that produced each decision. `GET /trace/:capture_id` returns it.
